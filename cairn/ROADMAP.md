- **Norms fitness and what the standardizing call tells the user** (2026-08-07, raised at the M74 gate): the shipped reference statistics are mostly convenience samples from single validation studies — 24 samples across 15 instruments, all American, 15 of them college students, seven with n < 300 — and the package calls them "norms", which implies a population referent most of them do not have. Measured at M74: reference-moment sampling error contributes only ~0.12 SD to a z-score at the worst shipped sample size (iipsc s2, n = 106) and ~0.035 at the largest (iei s1, n = 1223), but switching *which* sample of the same instrument you standardize against moves scores 0.44 SD on average and 0.78 at the extreme (iipsc college vs outpatient), unevenly across octants — so it changes SSM amplitude and displacement, not just elevation. Reference **choice** therefore dominates reference **precision** by 4–9x, which argues against any size threshold: the smallest shipped sample (n = 106 outpatients) is the only defensible referent for a clinical user, and a threshold would delete exactly it. The open work is naming and disclosure, not removal — what these statistics are called in docs (GP5 precision), and whether `norm_standardize()` should surface the sample and its n at the call site rather than leaving it to a `norms()` call the user must think to make. Distinct from the provenance-audit row above, which asks whether the values are *correct*; this asks whether their *use* is informed. May bear on v2.0.0, since part of it is what the documentation claims. Promote when the release walk reaches the docs, or on any report of a user standardizing against the wrong sample.
- **IIP T-score conversion tables** (2026-08-07, raised at the M75 gate): the IIP manual norms the IIP-32 and IIP-64 through per-scale T-score lookup tables (Appendices B and F), not through the M/SD z-scoring `norm_standardize()` implements. (This row's further claim that for the IIP-32 the tables are the *only* published norming content was **false and is corrected here (M75 plan, corrected M75)**: Table F.5, p. 91 prints the IIP-32 descriptives, and the shipped values reconcile against it exactly.) Adopting the tables was declined at the M75 gate on two grounds: the Mind Garden permission licenses "Normative Data (Means and SDs)" and a conversion table is neither, so shipping one needs a fresh permission request; and the printed tables are not the linear transform the manual's own p. 30 describes, so they are not equivalent to z-scoring. **Measured 2026-08-07 against the shipped M/SD, and the effect is small**: over the 408 IIP-32 cells not capped at the tables' 99 ceiling, published-minus-linear T has median 0.3 points (0.03 SD), 90th percentile 1.3, and maximum 4.0 (0.4 SD); below T = 60 it never exceeds 1.2 points. The divergence is a mild S-shape from the distribution's positive skew, confined to high scores — the headline gaps at raw 15–16 are the 99-ceiling, not disagreement. Octant-differential spread, which is what SSM shape sees, runs 0.10–0.23 SD in the normal range and ~0.44 SD only above raw 11 — smaller than the 0.44-average/0.78-extreme effect of choosing a different reference *sample* (norms-fitness row). Adopting the tables would therefore also be statistically backwards for this package: SSM fits a cosine to standardized scores on an interval metric, and a normalized T is a nonlinear monotone transform that distorts that structure. Promote only on a fresh permission covering the conversion tables *and* a concrete use case; a lookup-table norming path would also be a new estimand needing its own design pass.
