# nagy2019 — the figure conventions the visualization vignette's latent-circumplex section follows

**Provenance.** Ingested 2026-09-20 by M143 from
`cairn/references/sources/nagy2019.pdf` (gitignored).
Pagination: the journal's own, *Multivariate Behavioral Research* 54(3) 404–428; the shelf PDF's page *n* is printed page **403 + n** (25 PDF pages over 25 printed pages), so PDF p. 7 is printed p. 410.
Extraction: verified 2026-09-20 against the source by two channels — `pdftotext -layout` for the captions and the body text on pp. 406, 410, 415, 416, 421, 426 and 427, and a visual read of `pdftoppm`-rendered page images for the figure conventions on pp. 406, 410, 416 and 421; every anchor below names the channel that read it; the appendix formulas were not transcribed and are cited by heading and page only; the rest of the article was not read for this page, and no value here has been read by a human — observed 2026-09-20.

A figure convention — where a label sits, what a line style means — is a
rasterized read whatever the container (M42-D1): the text layer carries the
captions and none of the geometry. `pdfinfo` reports Producer `Zotero` with
a text layer that extracts cleanly (`=` comes out as `¼`, `−` as `` — the
font encoding, not OCR), so the two channels are independent.

**Citation.** Nagy, G., Etzel, J. M., & Lüdtke, O. (2019). Integrating
covariates into circumplex structures: An extension procedure for Browne's
circular stochastic process model. *Multivariate Behavioral Research,
54*(3), 404–428. https://doi.org/10.1080/00273171.2018.1534678

The PDF's own title metadata reads "Browneˇs" (a caron for the apostrophe);
the printed title uses an apostrophe.

**Role.** The model for the circle figures of `vignettes/advanced-visualization.Rmd.orig`
section 7 (M143) and for the cartesian canvas M142 added to
`coord_circumplex()` and `ggcircumplex()`. The repo takes from this source
**figure conventions only**: what its circle figures draw and how. Nothing
numeric is validated against it, and its SPMC-E model (the covariate
extension) is not implemented; that use is the "Covariate extension of the
CPM" candidate row, with the E1 entry of `forward-source-prospects.md`.

## Extracted values

### Figure 1, p. 406 — the canvas (caption: text channel; geometry: visual read)

- Caption: "Graphical representation of an equally spaced perfect
  circumplex (left panel), and the corresponding circulant correlation
  matrix (right panel)." The left panel is a plain circle with eight scale
  labels on the rim at the theoretical angles and no crosshair, ring or
  amplitude labels.
- Label format: the angle in parentheses beside the abbreviation, written
  `LM (0°)`, `NO (45°)`, `PA (90°)` on the right half and `(135°) BC`,
  `(180°) DE`, `(225°) FG`, `(270°) HI` on the left half; every label is
  rotated to its spoke direction except the two on the horizontal axis.
  `JK (315°)` on the lower right.

### Figure 3, p. 410 — one measure as a vector (caption: text channel; geometry: visual read)

- Caption: "Results of the SSM, CIRCUM-extension, and the preliminary
  extension of the SPMC used for placing the sociability scale (reverse
  coded) onto the Interpersonal Circumplex as assessed by the IIP-C. Left
  panels: Sample correlations (SSM) or rescaled covariances
  (CIRCUM-extension and SPMC extension) and model-implied correlations.
  Right panels: Vector representations of relationships."
- Right panels, all three: a circle of radius 1 with a horizontal and a
  vertical axis through the origin, each carrying tick marks and signed
  labels at ±0.2, ±0.4, ±0.6 (the labels sit beside the ticks); no rings and
  no spokes inside the circle. The scale labels sit on the rim, angle in
  parentheses, in the Figure 1 format.
- The SSM panel (top) places the labels at the theoretical angles, 45°
  apart; the two extension panels (middle, bottom) place them at the angles
  each model estimated — for example `(124°) PA`, `(135°) BC`, `NO (45°)`,
  `LM (0°)`, `HI (277°)` in the CIRCUM panel — so each model's rim shows
  only that model's angles.
- The measure is one filled point at its amplitude and displacement, joined
  to the origin by a straight line, with two text annotations: the
  displacement written `δ = 205°` (top panel) beside a short arc, and the
  amplitude written `γ̂₁ = 0.26` beside the line. The arc is drawn near the
  origin, from the 0° direction counterclockwise to the vector, and reads as
  the angle's definition rather than as an interval.
- The three panels' estimates: δ = 205°, γ̂₁ = 0.26 (SSM); δ = 234°,
  γ̂₁ = 0.32 (CIRCUM); δ = 233°, γ̂₁ = 0.33 (SPMC) — read from the rendered
  page at 160 dpi, the SSM panel's values confirmed on a 160-dpi crop, the
  other two read at 80 dpi only.

### Body text, p. 415 — what the four lines mean (text channel)

- "The first two lines mark the contact points of the tangents from the
  origin to the circumference of the ellipse. The tangents are represented
  as vectors with orientations δ̂q(1) and δ̂q(2), indicating the minimum and
  maximum values of orientations encapsulated by the confidence ellipse."
- "The second two lines indicate the points on the circumference of the
  confidence ellipse that are closest and farthest from the origin. The
  lengths of the corresponding vectors, γ̂q(1) and γ̂q(2), stand for the
  minimum and maximum distances of all points falling in the confidence
  ellipse to the origin".
- "Appendices B and C show how to compute the values of δ̂q(1), δ̂q(2),
  γ̂q(1), and γ̂q(2)."
- p. 415, on an ellipse that contains the origin: "the endpoints of all
  vectors encapsulated by the ellipse cover all directions from 0 to 2π".

### Figure 4, p. 416 — tangents and distances (caption: text channel; geometry: visual read)

- Caption: "Examples of confidence ellipses, associated tangents (black
  dotted lines), and minimum and maximum distances from the origin (gray
  dotted lines). Midpoints of ellipses all have an orientation of δq = 45°,
  but different distances to the origin (left panels: γ̂1,q = 0.3, right
  panels γ̂1,q = 0.6). All ellipses encapsulate the same area."
- Each panel is the first quadrant of the unit circle. The ellipse is a
  solid outline with a filled centre point; two black dashed lines run from
  the origin to the tangent contact points and stop there; two gray dotted
  lines run from the origin to the nearest and farthest boundary points.
- The four scenarios' printed values (text channel, each panel's header):
  Scenario 1 γ̂q(1) = 0.234, γ̂q(2) = 0.570, δ̂q(1) = 7.9°, δ̂q(2) = 84.6°;
  Scenario 2 0.534, 0.868, 22.5°, 68.4°; Scenario 3 0.113, 0.688, 16.5°,
  78.5°; Scenario 4 0.412, 0.988, 31.0°, 60.3°. The ellipses' covariances
  are not printed, so these values cannot serve as an oracle for the
  vignette's chunk.

### Figure 6, p. 421 — two measures with ellipses on the estimated rim (caption: text channel; geometry: visual read)

- Caption: "Upper panel: Location of admiration and rivalry scales on the
  IAS circumplex, including 95% confidence ellipses determined by the
  SPMC-E."
- The rim labels are the SPMC-E's estimated angles — `LM (0°)`, `NO (48°)`,
  `(98°) PA`, `(148°) BC`, `(180°) DE`, `(223°) FG`, `HI (272°)`,
  `JK (309°)` — with a short tick at each on the rim, and no theoretical
  angle drawn anywhere.
- Crosshair with gray labels 0.25, 0.50, 0.75 on the positive halves and
  −0.25, −0.50, −0.75 on the negative halves; no rings.
- Each measure: a filled point, its ellipse as a solid outline, its name as
  a text label beside the ellipse ("Admiration" above, "Rivalry" below), two
  black dashed lines from the origin to the tangent points and two gray
  dotted lines from the origin to the nearest and farthest boundary points.
  No line joins the origin to the point itself, and there is no wedge.

### Appendices B and C, pp. 426–427 (text channel, headings only)

- "Appendix B: Closest and farthest distance to the confidence ellipse"
  (p. 426): "a procedure for finding the closest and farthest distance from
  the origin of the coordinate system to the perimeter of the confidence
  ellipse, when the origin (0,0) of the coordinate system is outside the
  confidence ellipse." The distance is written as a function of the
  parametric angle on the ellipse and its extremes found from a quartic
  (eq. B8, four roots t1,q to t4,q).
- "Appendix C: Tangents of the confidence ellipse that pass through the
  origin" (p. 427): "The procedure requires that the confidence ellipse does
  not include the origin." A tangent through the origin is a line
  `y = ξ x` whose slope solves eq. C1.
- The vignette chunk `ellipse-lines` does not transcribe either appendix. It
  solves the same two problems in the covariance parameterization
  `geom_ssm_ellipse()` uses (a quadratic in the tangent direction; the
  boundary radius extremes over a fine parametric grid refined by
  `optimize()`), and is tested against a closed-form circular-ellipse oracle
  and the shipped outline, not against these formulas.
